# Primitive Isolation — Feature Ideas

## 1. Primitive Survival Systems

### 1.1 Fire Starting and Tinder
Make fire something players have to create rather than something they can always ignite instantly.

- Gather dry grass, sticks, and bark for tinder.
- Craft a hand drill or bow drill.
- Starting a fire takes time and may fail.
- Rain and wet materials make ignition harder.
- Flint and steel could become a later, more reliable method.

### 1.2 Gathering Wild Plants DONE - #6
Give players reasons to explore forests, plains, and riverbanks before farming.

- Gather wild barley, turnips, berries, yarrow, and poisonous bitterleaf from biome-specific patches; this feature does not use seasons.
- Replant harvested plants with their gathered item; use a digging stick to collect turnips.
- Identify plants with a field guide that records habitat, harvest method, and raw/prepared effects.
- Eating raw bitterleaf causes temporary poison; yarrow treats it, while a separate plant dryer prepares both herbs.

### 1.3 Baskets and Primitive Containers DONE
Create cheap storage before players have access to chests.

- Weave grass or reeds into baskets.
- Make small portable containers with limited capacity.
- Store gathered plants, sticks, stones, and food.
- Allow baskets to be placed in the world or carried.
- Add woven mats, simple sleeping mats, and rope.

### 1.4 Primitive Wood Processing
Make trees and wood more than a source of logs.

- Strip bark from logs for cordage, tinder, and tanning.
- Split logs into billets and planks using wedges.
- Carve sticks into stakes, handles, and digging tools.
- Use a wooden mallet and stone wedges to process larger pieces.
- Make wooden pegs and lashings for primitive structures.

### 1.5 Clay Gathering and Pottery
Introduce containers before the player can easily craft glass bottles or metal buckets.

- Dig clay from riverbanks or exposed deposits.
- Shape unfired pots and bowls.
- Dry them, then fire them in a pit or primitive kiln.
- Use pots for water, cooking, rendering fat, or storing salt.
- Unfired pottery breaks easily; fired pottery is more durable.

## 2. Primitive Technologies and Workstations

### 2.1 Grinding Stones and Querns
A manual processing system for plants and minerals.

- Grind wheat and other grains into flour.
- Crush seeds, dried plants, and salt-containing materials.
- Grind ores into crushed ore before smelting.
- Require repeated manual use instead of instant crafting.
- Later, introduce a powered mill as an upgrade.

### 2.2 Bone and Antler Crafting
Make animal remains useful beyond food and leather.

- Bones become needles, awls, fishing hooks, and small tools.
- Antlers become picks, handles, and knapping tools.
- Animal sinew becomes strong cordage.
- Bone needles unlock hide clothing and simple bags.
- Better processing tools improve yields from carcasses.

### 2.3 Fiber, Rope, and Lashings
Give plant fibers and animal sinew their own progression.

- Twist grass or plant fibers into cordage.
- Use sinew for stronger bindings.
- Make nets, fishing lines, traps, and bowstrings.
- Require rope or lashings to assemble certain tools and structures.
- Allow rope to secure ladders, rafts, and hanging storage.

### 2.4 Earth Ovens and Hot-Stone Cooking
Expand cooking beyond the campfire and furnace.

- Dig a cooking pit and line it with stones.
- Heat stones, then cook food slowly under a covering.
- Prepare roots, large cuts of meat, and batches of food.
- Make cooking take longer but consume less active player attention.
- Introduce stone types with different heat-retention properties.

### 2.5 Primitive Ore Processing
Create a multi-stage path from raw ore to usable metal.

- Collect ore-bearing rocks and break them with a hammerstone.
- Sort or wash crushed material.
- Build a clay-lined bloomery.
- Use charcoal and a bellows to smelt suitable ores.
- Hammer the resulting bloom into workable metal before unlocking advanced tools.
- Make metalworking a substantial milestone rather than a single furnace recipe.

## 3. Changes to Existing Vanilla Minecraft Mechanics

These changes could have more impact than adding dozens of new items.

### 3.1 Rework the First Few Minutes of a New World

- Hand-breaking logs could yield bark, twigs, or rough wood rather than a normal log.
- Require a crude cutting tool to harvest full logs efficiently.
- Allow sticks to be gathered from dead bushes, fallen branches, and leaf litter.
- Make early crafting use a flat stone, a rock, or a basic work surface.
- Keep vanilla recipes available through progression rather than removing everything permanently.

### 3.2 Change Mining and Tool Tiers

- Flint tools should be useful for wood and basic harvesting, but not automatically replace every vanilla tool.
- Introduce hammerstones for breaking rocks and processing ore.
- Require suitable tools to harvest certain blocks efficiently.
- Make mining unsuitable rock with a weak tool slow, ineffective, or yield less material.
- Add primitive prospecting: visible ore fragments or clues near deposits.

### 3.3 Rework Farming

- Make wild plants the first source of seeds.
- Require grain processing before wheat becomes useful flour.
- Add hand tools for digging and preparing soil.
- Let players dry seeds and store them for future planting.
- Consider seasonal growth or soil fertility as optional difficulty settings.

### 3.4 Rework Water Collection

- Make wooden bowls or pottery the earliest water containers.
- Reserve the vanilla bucket for a later metalworking stage.
- Add water skins made from prepared hide.
- Make collected water dirty in certain environments, requiring boiling or filtration.
- Let containers differ in capacity, durability, and portability.

### 3.5 Make Shelter Matter

- Add warmth loss in cold biomes and during storms.
- Make sleeping outdoors less effective than sleeping under a roof.
- Let bedding improve rest and recovery.
- Make wind and rain affect exposed campfires.
- Give primitive shelters a meaningful purpose before players build houses.

### 3.6 Rework Animal Interaction

- Make hunting yield meat, hides, bones, and other materials through butchering.
- Add snares for small animals and traps for selected wildlife.
- Require better enclosures and food to domesticate animals.
- Give animals fear responses, fleeing, and group behavior.
- Make animal husbandry a long-term alternative to constant hunting.

## 4. Additional Features

### 4.1 Fat Rendering
- Cook animal fat in a pot to make tallow.
- Use tallow for primitive lamps, waterproofing, or crafting.

### 4.2 Pitch and Resin
- Collect resin from suitable trees.
- Use it for waterproofing, adhesives, torches, and tool hafting.

### 4.3 Primitive Lamps
- Use tallow or oil in a small lamp.
- Give lamps limited burn time and less light than torches.

### 4.4 Wooden Shields
- Make a crude plank or hide-covered shield before metalworking.

### 4.5 Rainwater Collection
- Use bark, leaves, or crafted containers to collect rain.

### 4.6 Natural Dyes
- Extract dyes from plants, bark, and other natural materials.
- Use dyes for cloth and hide.

### 4.7 Hide Clothing
- Add simple boots, gloves, and clothing that improve warmth.
- Require multiple hide-processing stages to craft clothing.
